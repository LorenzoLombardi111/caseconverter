Title Case: The Standard for Headlines

Title Case capitalizes the first letter of every word and is the most common style for article headlines, book titles, and advertising copy. When a reader sees a Title Case headline, they immediately recognize it as a heading or title rather than body text. This visual distinction is critical in layouts where headings need to stand out from surrounding content.

Title Case works best for blog post titles, email subject lines, landing page headlines, ad headlines, course titles, and navigation menu labels. It is the default choice for most editorial and marketing contexts. If you are unsure which style to use, Title Case is a safe and widely accepted default.

Sentence Case: The Modern Alternative

Sentence case capitalizes only the first letter of each sentence, just like regular body text. It has become increasingly popular in tech writing, SaaS interfaces, and modern editorial design. Google, Apple, and Microsoft all use Sentence case in their product interfaces and documentation. Many blog platforms and content management systems now default to Sentence case headings.

Sentence case feels approachable, clean, and less formal than Title Case. It works well for subheadings within articles, UX microcopy, notification text, and brands that want a casual, conversational tone. If your audience is tech-savvy or your brand voice is informal, Sentence case may be the better fit.

AP Style vs Chicago Style Title Case

The two most influential English-language style guides handle title capitalization differently. Understanding the distinction helps you match your client's or publication's requirements.

Rule AP Style Chicago Style
Words with 4+ lettersAlways capitalizeAlways capitalize
Short prepositions (in, on, at)LowercaseLowercase
Articles (a, an, the)LowercaseLowercase
Conjunctions (and, but, or)LowercaseLowercase
First and last wordAlways capitalizeAlways capitalize
Prepositions 4+ letters (with, from)CapitalizeCapitalize
"Is," "are," "be" (short verbs)CapitalizeCapitalize
Hyphenated compoundsCapitalize first elementCapitalize both elements

For most copywriting work, the differences are subtle. The biggest practical distinction is hyphenated compounds: AP Style capitalizes only the first element ("Self-driving Cars"), while Chicago Style capitalizes both ("Self-Driving Cars"). When in doubt, follow your client's house style guide or default to AP Style, which is the most widely used standard in journalism and marketing.

Why Consistent Capitalization Matters for Ad Copy

In advertising, every visual detail communicates something to the reader. Inconsistent capitalization across ad variants makes your campaign look disjointed. A/B tests have shown that Title Case headlines can increase perceived authority, while Sentence case can increase perceived friendliness. The key is picking one style and applying it consistently across all creatives, landing pages, and email sequences in a campaign.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between AP Style and Chicago Style title case?

AP Style capitalizes all words with four or more letters and always capitalizes the first and last word. Short prepositions, conjunctions, and articles are lowercase unless they start or end the title. Chicago Style follows similar rules but differs on hyphenated compounds — Chicago capitalizes both parts while AP capitalizes only the first.

Should I use Title Case or Sentence case for blog post headlines?

Both are acceptable. Title Case is traditional for news articles and formal publications. Sentence case is increasingly popular for blog posts and tech writing because it feels more conversational. The most important thing is to be consistent across your entire site.

Why does capitalization matter for ad copy?

Capitalization affects readability, click-through rates, and brand perception. Title Case headlines tend to look more authoritative and professional. Sentence case headlines feel approachable and casual. Consistent capitalization across ad campaigns builds brand recognition.
