What case formats do writers use most?

Writers rely on a handful of capitalization formats throughout their work. Understanding when and why to use each style will save you time and keep your writing consistent across every project.

  • Title Case — Every Major Word Is Capitalized. Title case is the standard for headlines, blog post titles, article headings, book chapter names, and presentation slides. Most style guides (AP, APA, Chicago) require title case for formal headings, though the specific rules vary slightly between them.
  • Sentence case — Only the first word is capitalized. Sentence case is the natural capitalization style for body text, email subject lines, casual headings, and subtitles. Many modern style guides and content platforms now prefer sentence case for headings because it feels more conversational and reduces visual clutter.
  • UPPER CASE — Every letter is capitalized. Upper case is used sparingly for emphasis, acronyms, headers in certain editorial styles, legal documents, and design-heavy layouts. In digital writing, extended uppercase text can read as shouting, so writers typically reserve it for short phrases or titles.
  • lower case — No letters are capitalized. Lower case is used in certain design contexts, informal writing, social media captions, and stylistic branding. Some writers and publications deliberately use lowercase headings to create a casual, approachable tone.

What is sentence case?

Sentence case is a capitalization style where only the first letter of the first word in a sentence is capitalized, along with proper nouns. For example: "The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog." Sentence case is the most common capitalization style for body text, emails, and casual writing.

When should I use title case vs sentence case?

Title case (capitalizing every major word) is best for headlines, book titles, article headings, and formal document titles. Sentence case (capitalizing only the first word) is best for body text, subtitles, email subject lines, and informal headings. Many style guides now prefer sentence case for headings to reduce visual clutter.
